Event searches

The geometry finder, the gf calls, answers the question "when is this quantity equal to, above or below a value, or at an extremum?" Each search takes a confinement window, which holds the times to search, and returns the window on which the condition holds.

Define the search window

A Window is a sorted list of disjoint intervals. wninsd! adds an interval, wncard counts the intervals, and wnfetd returns one of them, counting from 1.

using Almanac

start = str2et("2025 MAR 18 00:00:00 TDB")
search = Window()
wninsd!(search, start, start + 2 * 86400.0)
wncard(search)
1

Choose the step

A search samples its quantity at the given step, then refines each change to the tolerance tol (s), whose default comes from gfstol and is 1 µs unless changed. A step that is too long misses events without warning. The step must be shorter than:

  • for a condition that holds or not ("=", "<", ">", an occultation, a field of view): the shortest interval on which the condition holds, and the shortest gap between two such intervals;
  • for an extremum ("LOCMIN", "LOCMAX", "ABSMIN", "ABSMAX"): the shortest interval on which the quantity is monotone.

Find eclipses

gfoclt finds when one body hides another from an observer. PROBE is in the shadow of the Earth when the Earth occults the Sun as seen from PROBE. The arguments are the occultation type ("ANY" for partial or full), the name, shape and body-fixed frame of the front body, the same for the back body, the correction, the observer, the step and the window. The first eclipse lasts less than two minutes, hence the step of 60 s; a step of 300 s can miss it.

eclipses = gfoclt("ANY", "EARTH", "ELLIPSOID", "IAU_EARTH",
    "SUN", "ELLIPSOID", "IAU_SUN", "LT", "PROBE", 60.0, search)

for i in 1:wncard(eclipses)
    t0, t1 = wnfetd(eclipses, i)
    println(timout(t0, "YYYY Mon DD HR:MN:SC ::TDB"), "   ", round(t1 - t0; digits=1), " s")
end
2025 Mar 18 21:15:37   107.1 s
2025 Mar 19 07:53:42   525.1 s
2025 Mar 19 18:33:34   727.5 s

Search on a coordinate

gfposc searches on one coordinate of a position, here a latitude above 20°. After the relation come the reference value, the adjustment (used only for absolute extrema) and the step. wnsumd[1] is the total duration.

north = gfposc("PROBE", "IAU_EARTH", "NONE", "EARTH", "LATITUDINAL", "LATITUDE",
    ">", 20 * rpd(), 0.0, 600.0, search)
wnsumd(north)[1] / 3600                     # hours
33.72183423539003

Search on a function of your own

gfuds searches on any scalar function of time. It also needs a function that tells whether the first one decreases at an epoch. uddc derives it from a numerical derivative over the given interval, here 10 s.

speed(t) = vnorm(spkezr("PROBE", t, "J2000", "NONE", "EARTH")[1][4:6])
fast = gfuds(speed, t -> uddc(speed, t, 10.0), ">", 8.0, 0.0, 600.0, search)
wncard(fast)                                # intervals with a speed above 8 km/s
5

Search on several threads

Every search takes the keyword threads=true, which processes intervals on separate threads.

  • Every search runs the intervals of its confinement window in parallel.
  • "=", "<", ">", and "ABSMIN" or "ABSMAX" with a nonzero adjustment, also run in parallel on the monotone intervals that they find first, so they gain even on a window of one interval.
  • Other extrema, occultations and field-of-view searches gain nothing on a window of one interval.

The functions given to gfuds or gfudb are then called concurrently, so they must be thread-safe.
