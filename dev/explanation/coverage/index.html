How the results were checked

CSPICE itself is the reference: the tests call each routine and its CSPICE counterpart (through SPICE.jl) on the same input and compare, rather than comparing with stored numbers. Inputs are NAIF kernels (planetary ephemerides, lunar orientation, leap seconds, constants) and kernels the tests write with the CSPICE writers, so that every data type is exercised.

Run NAIF's test suite

julia --project=test test/tspice/run.jl

The first run builds PEPPER with gfortran 16 and fpm, which must be on PATH, and records the calls of the about 400 tspice families into ~/.cache/almanac-tspice (about 2 GB, 15 to 40 minutes), with a manifest of the families and of the PEPPER commit. Later runs only replay them, in parallel processes. The recording keeps each distinct call of a routine without state once, and a query once between two changes of the loaded kernels or the settings; TSPICE_ALL=1 keeps every call.

A run without a list of families is the full check. It stops if a family is missing from the recording, failed to record, or fails to replay. It fails if a mismatch of value, of error kind, or of the script itself is not in the baseline (test/tspice/baseline_ids.tsv.gz): each mismatch is identified by family, routine, kind and a hash of its inputs, so a new mismatch fails even when another one went away. It also fails if a routine has more calls skipped or compared with a tolerance than test/tspice/coverage.tsv records, or fewer calls compared. The mismatches are in results/replay.tsv and results/ids.tsv. Each routine of test/tspice/baseline.tsv has a class and a note: documented, a difference that a docstring or this manual states; cspice, Almanac follows CSPICE where SPICELIB differs; gap, a call that the replay cannot reproduce, such as a read of a file that the test wrote with routines outside the recording; unexplained. --record records again, --update-baseline makes the full run the new baseline and keeps the classes, and a list of families (F_SPK08 F_CK06) restricts the run to them, without the coverage check. test/tspice/selftest.jl checks the gate itself: on a copy of the recording it removes a mismatch from the baseline, deletes a log, damages a log and marks a family as failed, and requires the run to fail each time.

Known limits of accuracy

Apart from the deliberate differences in Differences from CSPICE, results differ from CSPICE by rounding. For states of the Moon and the planets from de440s.bsp, in inertial and body-fixed frames, the default run measures:

QuantityAberration correctionsLargest relative difference from CSPICE
Position, velocity and light timeall nine (NONE to XCN+S)7.3e-16

Three limits hold in CSPICE as well, because they come from the size of a number or from the CSPICE method:

  • The rotation angle of the Earth in IAU_EARTH grows to about 50,000 rad this century; its rounding alone moves the Sun by some meters in that frame.
  • An aberration-corrected position has millimeter steps at the boundaries between SPK records, because target and observer are evaluated at different epochs and cross a boundary at different times.
  • spkezr and spkpos differ by up to 2e-11 relative with the converged corrections (CN, CN+S), because they stop iterating by different rules.

Beyond CSPICE

The calls of Orbits and relative motion are not CSPICE routines, so the accuracy figures above do not apply to them. They follow the source of ANISE main (commit b3aaadc) function by function, with its error and fallback cases; the released 0.10.6 and 1.0.0b0 differ from it in the flight path angle, inbound hyperbolas, the TNW frame and v-infinity. Where CSPICE computes the same quantity (oscltx, recgeo, conics, prop2b) it is the reference for the numbers; for everything else, and for what is an error or a fallback, the ANISE source is. Only the row named ANISE compares Almanac.jl with the output of ANISE.

Differences between two numbers are relative to the larger one unless a row says otherwise. The rows are tests of the group analysis_orbit; sampled inputs come from a fixed seed. The figures are those of the default run; in brackets follows the larger of two exhaustive runs (ALMANAC_TEST_FULL=1, seeds 20261011 and 1, which sample 20 to 35 times more), except in the row named ANISE. A difference of the angles or of the shape is given in units of the error model of the sampled orbit, because the rounding of an arc cosine or of 1 - e is amplified by the geometry of the sample: cond_shape is eps (1 + 1/|1 - e|), cond_angle is eps times the amplifier of the angle (1/ecc, 1/sin inc, 1/|sin angle|), cond_kepler is 1e-12 max(1, 1/sqrt(|1 - e²|)) (the stopping rule of the Kepler iteration) and cond_state is the larger amplifier of a rebuilt state (the definitions are in test/test_analysis_orbit.jl).

The row named ANISE is the opt-in group test/anise/ (julia --startup-file=no --project=test/anise test/anise/runtests.jl, which installs Python and the anise wheel through CondaPkg), run on 2026-11-15 against ANISE 1.0.0b0.

One defect of ANISE is reproduced, and three deviations from ANISE are deliberate:

  • Brouwer elements. They are those of the Earth: as in ANISE, a center other than body 399 throws SpiceError(:degeneratecase). For an inclination above 175° the iteration works on a pseudo-state, and ANISE maps back the inclination and raan but not the argument of periapsis; so does Almanac.jl, and :brouwer_aop then carries an error of about twice the short-period correction of that angle (0.6° for an orbit of 7200 km). This is ANISE's behaviour, reproduced, not corrected; the retrograde test case differs by 1.09e-2 rad in aop for that reason.
  • Hyperbolic two_body. ANISE recovers the mean anomaly after a step as the mean longitude minus atan2(h, k); for a hyperbola, whose mean anomaly has no period, that is wrong by a multiple of 2π when raan + aop lies outside (-π, π]. two_body advances the mean anomaly of a hyperbola directly, so there its states differ from those of ANISE and agree with prop2b (row prop2b); elliptical orbits use the formula of ANISE.
  • Frame rate. ANISE takes the rate of a local frame from the difference of two frames 1 ms apart. local_frame_state uses the exact rate of two-body motion, so it differs by the error of that difference quotient.
  • No NaN. Where ANISE returns NaN for an undefined quantity, the calls throw SpiceError(:degeneratecase); the few values that are returned instead (not finite, or a fallback angle) are in element and Element names.
