Numerical accuracy

This page says how closely the results of Almanac.jl follow those of CSPICE, where they differ, and how the derivatives of states and rotations are defined.

Agreement with CSPICE

Each routine follows the arithmetic of its CSPICE counterpart operation by operation, including the order of summation, and uses no fused multiply-add. With the same kernels and Float64 inputs, a result equals that of CSPICE bit for bit or differs by rounding. Tests and comparisons lists the checks, and Known limits of accuracy the measured differences.

The elementary functions (sin, cos, exp, ...) are those of Julia, while CSPICE calls the C library of the platform. Julia and glibc differ in the last bit for about 3 % of the arguments of sin and cos (measured on 10⁶ points between 1 and 51), so a result can differ from CSPICE in its last bit. Over long spans this grows: a type 5 SPK segment propagated over six years puts a planet barycenter 3e-8 km away from the position of CSPICE, at 2e8 km from the Sun. Geometry near a tangency amplifies it: in NAIF's test suite, a limb point on a DSK shape differs from that of CSPICE by up to 2.5e-3 km, and the intercept of a ray that grazes the surface by up to 4.4e-4 km. A change of the epoch by one unit in the last place moves the limb point of CSPICE itself by 3e-3 km. With the sin and cos of glibc in place of Julia's, these results equal those of CSPICE.

Derivatives and stored rates

Order 0 of spkder and pxder is the result of spkpos and pxform. Each higher order is the derivative of that result with respect to the epoch, with the SPK velocity in place of the derivative of the SPK position. For the SPK types whose velocity is the derivative of the position, such as type 2 of the planetary ephemerides, this is the exact derivative. For the types that store the velocity separately, such as type 9, order 1 of a geometric position is the stored velocity, as in spkezr, and so is the derivative that ForwardDiff returns for a dual-number epoch.

Derivatives of positions

With s = -1 for reception and s = +1 for transmission, spkder differentiates, in the barycentric inertial frame,

p(t) = r_targ(t + s ℓ(t)) - r_obs(t)
P(t) = R(t + s |pc(t)| / c) * A(p(t), s * v_obs(t))

where ℓ is the light time, A the stellar aberration and R the rotation from J2000 to the output frame.

  • With the LT corrections, ℓ(t) = |r_targ(t) - r_obs(t)| / c is the geometric light time, because the single iteration of CSPICE evaluates the target there.
  • With the CN corrections, ℓ solves ℓ(t) = |p(t)| / c, and its derivatives satisfy that equation order by order.
  • A is the identity without +S. Otherwise A(p, v) = p + |p| sinφ v̂⊥ + |p| (cosφ - 1) p̂, where v⊥ is the part of v perpendicular to p and sinφ = |v⊥| / c.
  • R is constant for an inertial frame. Otherwise its epoch is corrected for the light time to the center of the frame (the Earth for IAU_EARTH), and pc is p with that center as the target.

The velocity of spkezr differs from order 1 for two reasons:

  • Even for the LT corrections, which do not iterate to convergence, CSPICE takes the rate of the light time from the converged equation, which gives about 1e-8 relative for the Moon seen from the Earth (8.4e-9 measured with de440s.bsp).
  • For +S, CSPICE takes the acceleration of the observer from a difference of velocities ±1 s apart, which gives about 1e-13 relative.

For a body whose text PCK has cubic nutation and precession angles (BODY<sys>_MAX_PHASE_DEGREE = 3), the rate block of tisbod_ misses the cubic term, while tisbod, sxform and states in such frames give the exact derivative. With the Moon constants of the NAIF test suite, CSPICE differs from a central difference of tipbod by 1.1e-9 and Almanac.jl by 1.6e-13; degrees 1 and 2 agree to rounding.

Smoothness at record boundaries

For polynomial data (SPK types 2, 3, 8, 9, 12, 13, 14, 18, 20 and binary PCK), every order is the exact derivative of a polynomial of the segment: of the position polynomial, or, for the types that store the velocity separately, of the velocity polynomial from order 1 on. For other types and for frames defined by formulas, the algorithm is evaluated on truncated Taylor series, which gives its derivatives without finite differences.

Either way, a derivative is only as smooth as the data. At the boundary between two SPK records (the time spans of two sets of coefficients or states), the geometric position is continuous to rounding, and higher derivatives can jump. Aberration-corrected positions also have small steps there; see Known limits of accuracy.

Known limits of accuracy

For the states of the Moon and the planets from de440s.bsp, in inertial and body-fixed frames and with all nine aberration corrections (NONE to XCN+S), the largest relative difference of position, velocity and light time measured by the default test run was 7.3e-16.

Three limits hold in CSPICE as well, because they come from the size of a number or from the method of CSPICE:

  • The rotation angle of the Earth in IAU_EARTH grows to about 50,000 rad this century. Its rounding alone moves the Sun by some meters in that frame.
  • An aberration-corrected position has millimeter steps at the boundaries between SPK records, because target and observer are evaluated at different epochs and cross a boundary at different times.
  • spkezr and spkpos differ by up to 2e-11 relative with the converged corrections (CN, CN+S), because they stop iterating by different rules.
