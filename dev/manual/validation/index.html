Tests and comparisons

CSPICE is the reference. The tests call each routine and its CSPICE counterpart (through SPICE.jl) on the same input and compare the results, on NAIF kernels and on kernels written by the CSPICE writers so that every data type is exercised; the measured differences are in Known limits of accuracy. The orbit calls, which have no CSPICE counterpart, are compared with ANISE as well.

Comparisons with CSPICE

  • Files: a kernel written by Almanac.jl holds the data, word for word, of the one CSPICE writes from the same input. The bytes differ only where CSPICE leaves them unset.
  • Derivatives: the first derivative of a state is compared with the velocity of CSPICE, and each higher derivative with automatic differentiation of the one below.
  • Errors: an input that CSPICE refuses gives the kind of the CSPICE error. Checks that CSPICE does not make give kinds of Almanac.jl, such as :badarg.

NAIF's test suite

The calls of NAIF's test suite of CSPICE N0067 (tspice_c, the SPICELIB test suite translated to C) are recorded and replayed against Almanac.jl, and a replay fails on a difference that is not in the baseline, the list of known differences from CSPICE.

The orbit calls

The calls of Orbits and relative motion follow the source of ANISE function by function, including its error and fallback cases, with the differences from ANISE of the API reference. Where CSPICE computes the same quantity, CSPICE is the reference for the numbers; otherwise ANISE is. Differences below are relative unless a unit is given.

  • Against CSPICE (oscltx, recgeo, conics, prop2b): elements, geodetic coordinates, keplerian_orbit and two_body agree to a few multiples of the rounding error that the sampled orbit amplifies (through 1 - e near parabolic orbits, or through the arc cosine of a nearly circular or equatorial one).
  • Against published values: the GMAT, Vallado and Davis cases and the Brouwer, with_element and two_body cases of the ANISE tests pass within the limits of those tests. The largest difference is the B-plane of the GMAT hyperbolic case, 0.65 km (ANISE allows 1 km).
  • Against ANISE itself: every element, local frame and relative state of random elliptic, near-circular, near-equatorial, retrograde and hyperbolic states, with the same inputs given to both libraries. Most elements agree to 1e-11, angles of near-circular orbits to 3e-8, and frame rates to 1e-5, the error of the difference quotient that ANISE uses. This comparison is not part of the default test run.
  • Identities and derivatives: element identities, orthonormal local frames, the exact frame rate and the derivatives through the Kepler solver against ForwardDiff and closed forms; Enzyme, Mooncake, Zygote, ReverseDiff and Tracker against ForwardDiff.

Checks of every call

  • Threads: answers from many threads, while kernels are loaded and unloaded, equal those of one thread.
  • Allocation: AllocCheck.jl proves that the vector, matrix, coordinate and table calls, and the orbit calls element, keplerian, with_element, two_body, local_frame, local_frame_state and relative_state, cannot allocate for Float64 inputs. The tests measure the allocations of spkezr, spkpos, spkder, pxform, sxform and pxder call by call.
  • Package hygiene: Aqua.jl and ExplicitImports.jl.
