Validation

Almanac.jl follows the arithmetic of CSPICE N0067 operation by operation, in the same order of summation, and its tests call each routine and its CSPICE counterpart, through SPICE.jl, on the same input. The inputs include NAIF kernels and kernels written by the CSPICE writers, so that every data type is exercised. With de440s.bsp and all nine aberration corrections, states and light times agree bit for bit in inertial frames and to 4e-15 relative in body-fixed frames, and a kernel written by Almanac.jl holds the same data, word for word, as the one that CSPICE writes from the same input. NAIF's test suite of N0067 (tspice_c, the SPICELIB tests translated to C) is also replayed against Almanac.jl with the correctly rounded functions of CORE-MATH on both sides, and the replay fails on any difference from SPICELIB in the last bit that is not in its list of known differences and that CSPICE does not share.

Known limits of accuracy

The elementary functions (sin, cos, exp, ...) are those of Julia, whereas CSPICE calls the C library of the platform; for about 3 % of arguments, sin and cos differ in the last bit, and so can a result. The difference grows where a computation carries it over a long span, as in a type 5 SPK segment propagated over six years (3e-8 km at 2e8 km from the Sun), and where the geometry amplifies it, as at a limb point or a grazing intercept on a DSK shape: up to 2.5e-3 km in NAIF's test suite replayed with the functions of Julia, at a limb point that a change of the epoch by one unit in the last place moves by 3e-3 km in CSPICE itself. With the functions of CORE-MATH on both sides, these results are SPICELIB's to the last bit.

Three limits come from the size of a number or from the method, and hold in CSPICE as well:

  • The rotation angle of IAU_EARTH reaches about 50,000 rad this century, and its rounding alone moves the Sun by some meters in that frame.
  • An aberration-corrected position has millimeter steps at the boundaries between SPK records, which target and observer cross at different epochs.
  • spkezr and spkpos differ by up to 2e-11 relative with CN or CN+S, because they stop iterating by different rules.

Where CSPICE approximates a velocity or a rate, the derivative of Almanac.jl differs from it; the tutorial section Differences from spkezr and sxform gives each case.

Other checks

  • Errors: an input that CSPICE refuses gives the kind of the CSPICE error, and a check that CSPICE does not make throws an error kind of Almanac.jl, such as :badarg.
  • Derivatives: each order of a jet is compared with automatic differentiation of the order below, and every AD backend with ForwardDiff.
  • Threads: queries from many threads, while kernels are loaded and unloaded, give the answers of one thread.
  • Allocation: AllocCheck.jl proves that the vector, matrix, coordinate and table calls do not allocate on Float64 static-array inputs, apart from their error paths, and the tests measure the allocations of spkezr, spkpos, spkder, pxform, sxform and pxder call by call.
  • Package hygiene: Aqua.jl and ExplicitImports.jl.
