Validation of the orbit calls

The calls of Orbits and relative motion are not CSPICE routines, so the figures of Known limits of accuracy do not apply to them. They follow the source of ANISE main (commit b3aaadc) function by function, with its error and fallback cases. The released ANISE 0.10.6 and 1.0.0b0 differ from that source in the flight path angle, inbound hyperbolas, the TNW frame and v-infinity.

Where CSPICE computes the same quantity (oscltx, recgeo, conics, prop2b), CSPICE is the reference for the numbers. For everything else, and for what is an error or a fallback, the ANISE source is the reference. Only the check against ANISE compares Almanac.jl with the output of ANISE itself.

How to read the figures

  • Differences between two numbers are relative to the larger one, unless stated otherwise.
  • Each check is a test of the group analysis_orbit; sampled inputs come from a fixed seed.
  • The figures are those of the default run. Figures in brackets are the larger of two exhaustive runs (ALMANAC_TEST_FULL=1, seeds 20261011 and 1, which sample 20 to 35 times more); for the check against ANISE, they are one exhaustive run.
  • A difference of an angle or of the shape is given in units of the error model of the sampled orbit, because the geometry of the sample amplifies the rounding of an arc cosine or of 1 - e. The models are defined in test/test_analysis_orbit.jl:
    • cond_shape is eps (1 + 1/|1 - e|);
    • cond_angle is eps times the amplifier of the angle (1/ecc, 1/sin inc, 1/|sin angle|);
    • cond_kepler is 1e-12 max(1, 1/sqrt(|1 - e²|)), the stopping rule of the Kepler iteration;
    • cond_state is the larger of the amplifiers above, for a state rebuilt from its elements.

Against CSPICE

  • oscltx. sma, ecc, inc, raan, aop, ma, ta and period of 200 random orbits. In units of cond_shape: periapsis 3.2 [8.4], ecc 9.5 [16], sma and period 3.2 [5.3]. In units of cond_angle: inc 1.0 [1.8], raan 1.9 [2.0], aop 2.2 [3.3], ta 4.0 [6.0], ma 1.9 [4.8], aol 2.9 [3.0]. The larger raw differences of the anomalies are this amplification at the sampled orbits, not an error of a formula.
  • recgeo. latitude, longitude and height at 900 random points about three bodies, one of them flattened by 0.1: latitude 3.9e-16 rad, longitude 4.4e-16 rad, height 1.1e-15 of the equatorial radius.
  • conics. keplerian_orbit against the state of conics for 200 random orbits, and the round trip of the state. Position 1.2 [1.6] and velocity 0.96 [1.0] in units of cond_kepler; round trip of the state 8.8 [8.8] (cond_state), of sma and ecc 4.4e-15 [8.7e-15], of the angles 5.2 [9.1] (cond_angle).
  • prop2b. two_body over ±10 periods. Position 1.3 [3.2] and velocity 0.92 [5.5] in units of cond_kepler; sma 5.8e-15 [9.9e-15].

Against published values

  • GMAT cases. The Keplerian elements of three pairs of Cartesian and Keplerian states (circular inclined, elliptical, circular equatorial), the equatorial case, the two hyperbolic cases and 24 flight path angles, with the values that GMAT gave to the tests of ANISE; angles modulo 2π. Difference scaled by max(|value|, 1): at most 4.0e-13 (the limit is 1e-12); flight path angles 2.2e-16.
  • GMAT B-plane. bplane_bt, bplane_br, bplane_mag and bplane_angle of one hyperbolic case. Absolute difference from GMAT: components 0.65 km, magnitude 0.23 km, angle 2.0e-5 rad. The test of ANISE allows 1 km and 0.01°. The cause is not isolated.
  • Vallado. Longitude, geodetic latitude, height and the altitudes of Examples 3-3 and 3-4 of Vallado, with the radii that ANISE uses. Absolute: 2.2e-16 rad (latitude), 1.1e-16 rad (longitude), 2.1e-12 km (height), 4.2e-12 km (altitudes).
  • Davis. The hyperbolic anomaly of the state of the lecture of Davis, as ANISE tests it, and vinf_periapsis as the inverse of vinf_turn_angle: hyperbolic anomaly 1.1e-11 rad, inverse 1.1e-13 km.
  • Test cases of ANISE. The round trips of Brouwer mean elements (osculating, mean, osculating), with_element and with_apsides on the loops of verif_with_init, and two_body on verif_orbit_at_epoch, with the inputs and limits of the tests of ANISE:
    • Brouwer, low orbit: sma 3.1e-11 km, angles up to 3e-11 rad;
    • Brouwer, transfer orbit: sma 2.4e-9 km;
    • Brouwer, retrograde orbit: sma 3.0e-3 km, ecc 4.1e-7, inc 3.8e-6, raan 4.3e-4, aop 1.09e-2, ma 3.6e-5 rad; the aop figure is a defect of ANISE, reproduced (see Deviations from ANISE);
    • with_element and with_apsides: at most 1.5e-11;
    • verif_orbit_at_epoch: aop 3.3e-8 rad (ANISE allows 5.2e-8).

Against ANISE

Every element, local frame and relative state of 12,000 random states about the Earth and Mars (2000 each of elliptic, near-circular, near-equatorial, retrograde, outbound hyperbolic and inbound hyperbolic), and keplerian_orbit, with_element, with_apsides and two_body on the same states, with the same state, epoch, GM and radii given to both libraries. In brackets, one exhaustive run of 120,000 states. Differences are scaled by max(|value|, 1), angles are taken modulo 2π, and the range convention of every angle is checked.

  • Most elements: at most 1.1e-12, reached by period [tlong, 8.4e-12]. light_time: 1.0e-9 s, because ANISE rounds a duration to 1 ns.
  • ta, aop, ma, ea, and the same angles after with_element or with_apsides: 2.2e-8 [2.7e-8], at the near-circular states (ecc down to 1e-9), where the arc cosine amplifies rounding.
  • Brouwer elements: 2.6e-10 [3.6e-10], worst at a near-equatorial state.
  • Local frames (local_frame) and the position of relative_state: identical.
  • keplerian_orbit 4e-14 [1.3e-12], with_element 1.1e-11 [4.8e-11], with_apsides 1.4e-8 [1.2e-8].
  • two_body: 3.5e-9 [7.2e-9] for orbits that are not hyperbolic, 3.2e-11 [8.3e-11] for hyperbolas with raan + aop inside (-π, π].
  • Rates of the local frames and velocity of relative_state: 1.3e-6 [8.0e-6]. This is at most 1.5 times 1e-12/(2 ms), the error that the stopping rule of the Kepler iteration of ANISE (1e-12 rad) gives to its 1 ms difference quotient, and 0.02 times that for outbound hyperbolas.

Not compared, because 1.0.0b0 defines them differently from the source that Almanac.jl follows: fpa, vinf_periapsis, vinf_turn_angle, the inbound hyperbolic results of the constructors and of the rates, the TNW frame, and the B-plane calls (absent in 1.0.0b0). Reported but not tested: two_body and the rates of hyperbolas with raan + aop > π, a deliberate deviation described below.

This check is the opt-in group test/anise/, run on 2026-11-15 against ANISE 1.0.0b0. It installs Python and the anise wheel through CondaPkg:

julia --startup-file=no --project=test/anise test/anise/runtests.jl

Internal checks

  • with_element. The element that is set, and the five that must not change, over random orbits. The set sma (relative) or ecc: 13 [26] units of cond_shape; the set angle: 2.8 [6.6] units of cond_angle. The others: sma or ecc 1.4e-14 [3.3e-14], angles 6.0 [14].
  • Identities. aol = aop + ta, c3 = 2 energy, mean_motion, ta_dot, right ascension and declination, the hyperbolic functions of ha, the B-plane and v-infinity formulas, the conversion back from equinoctial elements, relative_state(o, o) == 0, orthonormal local frames, and the inverse of local_frame_state:
    • scalar identities: at most 1.6e-15 [4.4e-15];
    • B-plane and v-infinity: 1.2e-15 [5.5e-15];
    • aol: 2.9 [3.0] units of cond_angle;
    • equinoctial conversion: 0.79 [1.5] units of eps (16 + 1/sin inc);
    • orthonormality: 4.4e-16 [8.9e-16].
  • Derivatives with ForwardDiff.
    • The exact rate of local_frame_state against ForwardDiff.derivative of the frame of two_body: 6.9 [51] in units of eps |h|/r², the angular rate of the frame.
    • The derivative of the state of two_body with respect to a Dual epoch, against the velocity and acceleration of two-body motion: 3.7e-15 [8.6e-15] for the position, 3.8e-15 [9.2e-15] for the velocity.
    • The derivatives of the true anomaly through the Kepler solver (Dual mean anomaly and eccentricity, nested for the second derivative) against their closed forms: dν/dM 9.0e-15 [1.5e-14], dν/de 1.7e-15 [3.9e-15], d²ν/dM² 2.7e-12 [7.7e-12] relative to its bound.
  • Other AD packages against ForwardDiff. Enzyme (forward and reverse), Mooncake (forward and reverse), Zygote, ReverseDiff and Tracker, in the groups ad_enzyme, ad_mooncake, ad_zygote and ad_tracked. The default run compares the gradients of keplerian_orbit with respect to the elements (ellipse, hyperbola, and mean anomaly π, the branch of the Kepler solver where ANISE returns E for ν) and the derivative of the local frame of orbit with respect to the epoch. The exhaustive tier adds two_body; the elements sma, ecc, inc, raan, aop, ta, ma, latitude, bplane_bt and brouwer_sma; local_frame; relative_state with respect to the state; and :sma of orbit with respect to the epoch. Results: at most 1.6e-15 for the gradients of the default run and 2.1e-15 over all cases (:ta with Tracker), and 1.3e-14 for :sma of orbit with respect to the epoch. The default limits are 5e-14 for the gradients and 1e-13 for the epoch.
  • Allocation. AllocCheck.jl proves, for Float64 inputs, that element, keplerian, with_element, two_body, local_frame, local_frame_state and relative_state cannot allocate. keplerian_orbit, with_apsides, Orbit and orbit are not in the proof.

Deviations from ANISE

One defect of ANISE is reproduced, and three deviations from ANISE are deliberate.

  • Retrograde Brouwer elements (the defect reproduced). As in ANISE, Brouwer elements are those of the Earth, and a center other than body 399 throws SpiceError(:degeneratecase). For an inclination above 175° the iteration works on a pseudo-state, and ANISE maps back the inclination and raan but not the argument of periapsis. Almanac.jl does the same, so :brouwer_aop then carries an error of about twice the short-period correction of that angle (0.6° for an orbit of 7200 km). This behaviour of ANISE is reproduced, not corrected.
  • Hyperbolic two_body. After a step, ANISE recovers the mean anomaly as the mean longitude minus atan2(h, k). For a hyperbola, whose mean anomaly has no period, this is wrong by a multiple of 2π when raan + aop lies outside (-π, π]. two_body advances the mean anomaly of a hyperbola directly, so there its states differ from those of ANISE and agree with prop2b. Elliptical orbits use the formula of ANISE.
  • Frame rate. ANISE takes the rate of a local frame from the difference of two frames 1 ms apart. local_frame_state uses the exact rate of two-body motion, so it differs from ANISE by the error of that difference quotient.
  • No NaN values. Where ANISE returns NaN for an undefined quantity, the calls throw SpiceError(:degeneratecase). (A derivative can still be NaN; see Dual numbers.) The few values that are returned instead (not finite, or a fallback angle) are listed in element and Element names.
