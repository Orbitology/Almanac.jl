Validation of the orbit calls

The calls of Orbits and relative motion are not CSPICE routines. They follow the source of ANISE main (commit b3aaadc) function by function, including its error and fallback cases. The released ANISE 1.0.0b0 differs from that source in the flight path angle, inbound hyperbolas, the TNW frame and v-infinity.

Where CSPICE computes the same quantity, CSPICE is the reference for the numbers; otherwise ANISE is. Differences below are relative unless a unit is given.

What is checked

  • Against CSPICE (oscltx, recgeo, conics, prop2b): elements, geodetic coordinates, keplerian_orbit and two_body agree to a few multiples of the rounding error that the sampled orbit amplifies (through 1 - e near parabolic orbits, or through the arc cosine of a nearly circular or equatorial one).
  • Against published values: the GMAT, Vallado and Davis cases and the Brouwer, with_element and two_body cases of the ANISE tests pass within the limits of those tests. The largest difference is the B-plane of the GMAT hyperbolic case, 0.65 km (ANISE allows 1 km).
  • Against ANISE itself: every element, local frame and relative state of random elliptic, near-circular, near-equatorial, retrograde and hyperbolic states, with the same inputs given to both libraries. Most elements agree to 1e-11, angles of near-circular orbits to 3e-8, and frame rates to 1e-5, the error of the difference quotient that ANISE uses (see below).
  • Identities and derivatives: element identities, orthonormal local frames, the exact frame rate and the derivatives through the Kepler solver against ForwardDiff and closed forms; Enzyme, Mooncake, Zygote, ReverseDiff and Tracker against ForwardDiff.
  • Allocation: AllocCheck.jl proves that element, keplerian, with_element, two_body, local_frame, local_frame_state and relative_state cannot allocate for Float64 inputs.

The comparison with ANISE is an opt-in group, which installs Python and the anise wheel through CondaPkg:

julia --startup-file=no --project=test/anise test/anise/runtests.jl

Deviations from ANISE

  • Retrograde Brouwer elements (an ANISE defect, reproduced). Above 175° of inclination ANISE maps back the inclination and raan from its pseudo-state but not the argument of periapsis, so :brouwer_aop carries an error of about twice its short-period correction (0.6° for a 7200 km orbit). Brouwer elements are those of the Earth; another center throws SpiceError(:degeneratecase).
  • Hyperbolic two_body. ANISE recovers the mean anomaly as the mean longitude minus atan2(h, k), which is off by a multiple of 2π for a hyperbola when raan + aop lies outside (-π, π]. two_body advances the mean anomaly of a hyperbola directly and agrees with prop2b.
  • Frame rate. ANISE differences two frames 1 ms apart; local_frame_state uses the exact rate of two-body motion.
  • No NaN values. Where ANISE returns NaN, the calls throw SpiceError(:degeneratecase). (A derivative can still be NaN; see Dual numbers.) The few non-finite or fallback values that are returned are listed in element and Element names.
